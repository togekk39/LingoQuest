The chapter has 18 formal stages. The market likes mission teaches foods, drinks, statements and questions in four stages. Quantity uses four stages for units, portions, uses and questions. Four price stages move from currency to larger spoken numbers, questions and answers. The café's six stages cover menu expressions, menu vocabulary, ingredients, substitutions, confirmation and repair. The seaside mission draws on these prerequisites. The material IDs for the four core missions are `u03_l01`, `u03_l02` and `u08_l01`, while the evidence targets distinguish each exact skill and updated prompt. ## Story and assets

The Mexico City postcard opens Cartagena. The player travels with Sam and Luz as a separate character. Mar sells food at the market. Alba serves at the restaurant and returns for the seaside exchange. [Mission source](../content/source/cartagena-missions.ts) · [Scene and dialogue source](../content/source/cartagena-story.ts)

| Mission                                        | Player action                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `cartagena-likes` · El mercado de Cartagena    | Read the arrival postcard, meet Mar and use market conversations and profiles to learn what each person likes.                          |
| `cartagena-quantity` · ¿Cuánto necesitamos?    | Open the basket activity and choose enough food and drinks for the group.                                                               |
| `cartagena-prices` · ¿Cuánto cuesta?           | Read the café menu board and hear prices in Colombian pesos.                                                                            |
| `cartagena-order` · Una mesa para tres         | Read the menu and ingredient options. Place an order, confirm the selected combination, correct Sam’s drink and ask for the check.      |
| `cartagena-transfer` · Una comida junto al mar | Use a different menu and diner constraints with Alba. Complete the exchange to earn the Cartagena stamp and collect the Cusco postcard. |

The main interaction targets are `cartagena-street-postcard`, `cartagena-market-mar`, `cartagena-market-basket`, `cartagena-cafe-menu-board`, `cartagena-cafe-alba` and `cartagena-seaside-alba`. The final dialogue awards the inventory item `cartagena-cusco-postcard`. The reply to a submitted order follows the selected dish and drink, including an explicit cup, glass or bottle when the player names one. [Scene and dialogue definitions](../content/source/cartagena-story.ts) · [Order variants](../content/source/cartagena-assessments.ts)

The story uses fictional venues, menus and prices. Its Caribbean setting and colonial streets draw on Cartagena's documented cityscape. The food examples reflect local egg arepas and fried fish with coconut rice and patacones (fried plantains). The menu uses Colombian pesos (`COP`). ## Grading, remediation and challenge

| Outcome                 | Example and evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Target form and context | In the fish-and-rice scenario, `I'd like fish with rice and some water, please.` meets the food, drink and form conditions. It can contribute independent writing evidence when the preparation and response conditions are met. |
| Communication only      | `Can I have fish with rice and some water, please?` conveys the request when the item specifically targets `I'd like`. It records successful communication.                                                                      |
| Context mismatch        | `I'd like eggs with cheese and some water, please.` uses the requested grammatical frame and misses the food or ingredient condition.                                                                                            |
| Incorrect expression    | `I like fish with rice and some water.` states a preference during an order task. Feedback returns to the required request.                                                                                                      |
| Self-check              | Open oral or written practice invites an attempt and then offers observable criteria and an optional model. The ledger records practice and support.                                                                             |

Remediation associates an explanation and guided practice with a fresh independent confirmation on the same target and dimension. Guided success leaves the error pending until a qualifying independent response. Grammar Rush, Cloze Battle and Reading Quest use the existing ranks and require exact formal teaching and normal independent practice for the relevant skill. Same-day success across eligible contexts can establish transfer. Retention requires a delayed independent response on a different prompt. Each keyed item in the audit has an accepted and relevant rejected or context-mismatch example checked with the shared offline grader.
