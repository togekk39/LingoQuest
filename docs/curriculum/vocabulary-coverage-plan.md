## Lexical progression

A tracked entry is a **lemma with a specified sense** or a **multiword expression with a specified function**. Record inflected and contracted forms under that entry, with the forms actually taught. Mark recognition and productive use separately by modality. The focal column counts comma-separated New entries in the master plan (including grouped inflections or expressions), before sense-level editing. Reproduce these counts with `npm run curriculum:verify -- --inventory`. The 425 focal entries describe authoring scope. The wider cumulative ranges are provisional capacity estimates for taught and recycled entries in core and companion materials. Use them to estimate editorial work and review load. The number of entries has no role in awarding a CEFR level.

### Entry and recycling rules

- Maintain an entry ID, base form, part of speech or construction, sense or function in es-419, inflection and contraction inventory, pronunciation/stress, register, collocations, an en-US example with translation, first teaching step and later lesson IDs. Homographs and different senses get distinct IDs. For `see`, label visual perception separately from understanding an explanation. Link `see`, `saw` and `seen` to their actual teaching steps. For a collocation such as `raise a concern`, track the useful combination in addition to the constituent words.
- Teach in short, meaningful sets. Start companion planning with about 4–7 focal entries per lesson where appropriate and budget another 3–8 per supported companion session only when they serve its communicative task. Separate items selected for unaided production from those for recognition. Review the candidate entries against learner relevance, previous coverage and capacity for delayed practice. Use a smaller batch if retention, text coverage or completion deteriorates. Multiple encounters with one sense increase exposure and leave the inventory count unchanged.
- Teach before independent use. Associate each important sense or chunk with an immediate supported encounter, an early retrieval around day 1 and a parallel retrieval around day 7. Reuse across a different speaker, text or purpose and in a later unit. Use word/form/meaning links and practice each required skill with suitable media. Record spacing, help and independent outcomes in the existing review model. Disclose gaps where an independent or oral check is unavailable. [Skill Dependency Map](skill-dependency-map.md) · [Kim & Webb, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479)
- Inspect estimated known **running-word coverage** for each text and important sense or multiword expression separately. For fluency input, aim toward the high end of 95–98% familiar running words after an editorial check. Adapt or scaffold lower-coverage teaching texts and mark support. For unassisted language in varied genres, Nation's corpus estimates illustrate why broader exposure is needed beyond the proposed tracked inventory. Use learner comprehension and evidence of inference to calibrate a text. Corpus coverage alone does not establish comprehension or CEFR level. [Nation, 2006](https://doi.org/10.3138/cmlr.63.1.59) · [Nation, 2007](https://doi.org/10.2167/illt039.0)

## Home foundation and number reuse

Home introduces greetings, instruction and repair expressions, names, places, objects, descriptions and directions in short scenes. Alphabet names and their commonly confused contrasts are taught with uppercase and lowercase print and English recordings. Letter names and sound contrasts are tracked as distinct teaching material. Chunks such as `Can you spell that, please?` can be used after phrase-level teaching. Their syntax can be introduced later as needed. [Home objective and audio audit](../PHASE_5D_HOME_FOUNDATION_VALIDATION.md)

Number instruction starts with 0–10, then covers 11–19, the tens and 100. Teach the tens-plus-units pattern in context. Use bus, seat and room numbers, quantities, price, age, phone digits and time to show different uses of the same number vocabulary. Home and Mexico City share `lex.number.<n>` for equivalent spoken number words. Record the exact required form and assess the applicable ability separately. Phone digit grouping and reading a time are distinct skills. [Shared number inventory](../../content/source/travel-number-words.ts) · [Home item audit](../PHASE_5D_HOME_FOUNDATION_VALIDATION.items.tsv)

## Input and output specifications for authoring pilots

The ranges below are LingoQuest starting points for drafts, including short/long variants as appropriate. They operationalize the progression from short, predictable reception and exchanges to connected comprehension, sustained expression, interaction and mediation. CEFR scales describe what a learner can do under conditions. They do not prescribe word counts or speech rates. Count speech rate over voiced material and natural pauses. Document rate, pause placement, number of speakers, accent, critical points and replay rules. Establish actual difficulty in parallel forms with new learners. [CEFR Companion Volume, chapter 3, reception, production, interaction and mediation](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4)

Accents and venue names are never shorthand for difficulty. Teach models in en-US. In receptive tasks widen accent exposure deliberately, including international English and speakers with diverse first languages, and check that the target stays the same across voices. Control vocabulary, context, accent novelty and rate separately when testing progression. Make transcripts, slower playback and accessible controls available and record consequential support in the evidence. Use alternate stimuli when the delivery method changes the skill being observed.

## Study time and practice load

Cambridge English's cumulative guided-learning estimates include lessons and supervised study. Use them as external capacity benchmarks. The Pre-A1 allocation is a LingoQuest pilot assumption within the A1 total. The per-band amounts below are rounded planning centers, not additive promises or tests. Count actual time spent in guided activities, autonomous reading/listening, interaction, revision and review separately. Prior learning, literacy, age, contact with English, accessibility, device use and feedback availability can change the pace. [Cambridge English guided learning hours](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours)

| Band   | Guided hours from start · planning center for the band | Starter week to pilot, adjustable to fit the learner                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pre-A1 | 15–30 provisional · about 20                           | 3–5 short sessions of 10–20 min, including two sound/print practices, two brief exchanges and three spaced reviews of 3–5 min.                                                                              |
| A1     | 90–100 benchmark · about 75 beyond Pre-A1              | 4–6 sessions of 20–35 min, including at least two fresh inputs, two short outputs across oral and written modes, and two review opportunities.                                                              |
| A2     | 180–200 benchmark · about 95 beyond A1                 | 4–6 sessions of 30–45 min, including two short audios/text pairs, one unscripted service scenario, one message with revision and spaced review.                                                             |
| B1     | 350–400 benchmark · about 185 beyond A2                | 4–6 sessions of 35–55 min, with two connected reading/listening tasks, two outputs with one oral interaction and one connected written task, and a fluency/review session.                                  |
| B2     | 500–600 benchmark · about 175 beyond B1                | 4–6 sessions of 45–60 min, with at least two extended inputs across reading and listening, one discussion with a real response, one source-based writing and revision cycle, and delayed transfer practice. |

The weekly entries are workload examples, with review inside sessions where useful. They describe a repeatable rhythm across many weeks. Budget time for meaning-focused input, output, focused language study and fluency, then adjust using observed learning and time-on-task. A pilot should sample lexical retention, coverage by genre, transfer and the amount of usable oral/written feedback. Set minimum coverage per unit based on those observations. The 83 core lessons can organize this work. Today the number of available graded texts, sustained recordings, feedback-bearing interactions and delayed variants is too small to claim a complete B2 learning path. Commission the collections against the gaps in the [coverage matrix](cefr-coverage-matrix.md). [Nation, _The Four Strands_](https://doi.org/10.2167/illt039.0)
