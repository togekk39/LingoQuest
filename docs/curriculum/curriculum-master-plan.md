## Scope and entry points

The proposed core has **83 lessons in 24 units across 20 stops**. A lesson is a communicative objective taught over one or more resumable sessions. The count excludes recurring practice, longer input, delayed checks and optional supported enrichment. Lessons separate objectives when a new prerequisite or a different kind of evidence warrants it. Pilots can change the count.

| A1     | 5     | 22      | Home through Salvador, `lq-home-plaza.origins` (lesson 5)           |

The route is the recommended sequence. Start at Pre-A1 without prior English, or use a low-stakes diagnostic and a sample output to recommend a starting point for each skill. Earlier material remains available. A learner may sample later content or follow the story with support. A milestone records the skills actually observed.

The Council of Europe's Companion Volume grounds the pathway in reception, production, interaction and mediation. The stop labels serve as course levels. Each lesson specifies an observable outcome in each of four language skills. Band exits sample several descriptor scales and report a profile by skill. ## How to use each lesson specification

- The unit supplies the stop, story scene and a distinct transfer setting. The **S** or **W** goal gives the communication task in that scene. The outcome across the four goals is the lesson objective. A learner can do the same lesson in Study using its scene brief.
- **Prerequisites** name prior targets by stable lesson ID and suffix. **form** means the listed construction or discourse strategy. **l/s/r/w** mean listening, speaking, reading and writing. **New** and **Review** list focal en-US entries, with inflections grouped using an en dash. Teach set phrases with their contextual sense. At Pre-A1 introduce written forms and sounds together. Teach additional required words by stage or supply an appropriate gloss before offering supported practice. For independent checks use taught items and permit level-appropriate inference without scoring ancillary language as a target.
- Each lesson has guided practice on its form and words, followed by a parallel independent task for each of **L**, **S**, **R** and **W**. Use the formats and difficulty below. The concrete details, actions and conditions in each goal are required for that mode's local check. For example, the `place` listening check requires finding the correct stop through two landmarks. Its speaking task requires both a question and a usable location.
- **Review mapping** uses `<lesson-id>:lex` (resolved to lexical senses) and `<lesson-id>:{form,l,s,r,w}` for the stated aims. Resolve these references to reusable canonical targets at authoring time. Rehearse an analogous task in the listed transfer setting after roughly 1 day and again after 7 days, with different prompts and contexts. Mix the target into later units when the same construct recurs. Use the due-review schedule after the second check. [Skill Dependency Map](skill-dependency-map.md)

| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pre-A1, very short and familiar           | Picture or sound matching, same/different, two-option discrimination, copy then retrieve, and a short spoken exchange. Independent check uses short utterances or labels, a new name/code, a short reply and a single field or line.                                   |
| A1, concrete with simple sequences        | Map or timetable choices, short dictation, sentence building and guided role-play. ## Lesson checks and band exits

Use two opportunities on fresh parallel prompts before offering a new practice set. Each check uses a per-mode key and marks task-critical conditions in advance. For listening and reading, score at least three of four meaning points (two of three at Pre-A1) and every critical point. Longer tasks use several questions or an evidence grid. In speaking and writing, score **task fulfillment**, **comprehensibility**, **control of the stated form or discourse strategy** and **organization or interaction** separately on 0–2 scales. At least 1 on each required dimension, 2 on task fulfillment, and no missed critical condition pass the local check. A 1 means the aim is observable with some error that does not block communication. A 2 means it is consistently clear in this task. At Pre-A1, score fulfillment and comprehensibility and track the fixed expression as coached practice until separately observed. Accept natural equivalents and score the intended form only when demonstrated. When a mode is unavailable or has only self-assessment, show the result as unobserved or practice. The lesson can still be completed.

Every band exit uses independent evidence beyond lesson checks, story rewards and placement. Sample on entry, after relevant study and after a delay. In addition to teaching activities, use two parallel forms across different settings, one at least seven days after initial success. Each form samples listening for gist/details, reading for information and purpose, spontaneous spoken exchange and sustained speech appropriate to the band, and an independent written response. At B1 and B2 include mediation that preserves essential source information. Use trained raters and anchored examples for open performance, check agreement on a double-rated sample, and pilot cut scores by skill and form. The lesson thresholds are provisional. Build longer exposure in the same subject areas. The B1 project unit includes multi-speaker updates, a narrative with clarification and editor-reviewed independent reading. B2 includes a public explainer or talk, an editorial paired with an informational text, and discussion with changing positions. Use varied speakers and provide sustained writing and interactive feedback. Pilot hours, lexical sense coverage, task variety, learning gains and retention before setting a pace or claiming sufficient implementation coverage. The 83 core specifications define planned coverage. Formal CEFR claims require a validated assessment, documented standard setting and an appropriate issuing body.

## Lesson specifications

### `lq-home-start` · First contact (`home`, Pre-A1)

**Story** · Sam arrives at the plaza and needs a point of contact.

**Transfer** · A visitor at a school or community open house.

#### `lq-home-start.greet` · Greet a visitor

- **Prerequisites** · —
- **New** · hi, hello, goodbye, please, thank you
- **Review** · —
- **Form and patterns** · Greetings and leave-taking as chunks: Hi. Goodbye. Thank you.
- **Guided practice** · Sort hello and goodbye clips, then role-play an arrival and a departure with picture cues.
- **L** · Distinguish two greetings from two farewells.
- **S** · Greet and say goodbye to Sam in two turns.
- **R** · Match two labels to arrival and departure.
- **W** · Put one appropriate greeting on a welcome card.

#### `lq-home-start.name` · Give your own name

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-start.greet:s`
- **New** · I, you, my name is, I am
- **Review** · hi, hello
- **Form and patterns** · Fixed first-person frames before agreement: I'm [name] / My name is [name].
- **Guided practice** · Match greeting cards to voices, assemble a name frame, then exchange names with a partner.
- **L** · Identify the name in two slow introductions.
- **S** · Greet Sam and give your own or chosen name.
- **R** · Locate a name on a card without relying on a picture.
- **W** · Write your name using one of the two frames.

#### `lq-home-start.spell` · Check letters and digits

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-start.name:s`
- **New** · spell, letter, number, zero, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine
- **Review** · my name is, thank you
- **Form and patterns** · Alphabet A–Z in three small practice sets; How do you spell…? / [digit] as an ID code.
- **Guided practice** · Use sound-to-letter matching, digit dictation and a two-field registration role-play.
- **L** · Distinguish B/V and E/I and recover a three-digit code.
- **S** · Spell a chosen first name and confirm the last digit.
- **R** · Locate a matching name and a room code in a two-entry list.
- **W** · Transcribe four letters and three digits in separate fields.

#### `lq-home-start.repair` · Ask for a repeat

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-start.spell:l`, `lq-home-start.greet:s`
- **New** · again, slowly, sorry, yes, no
- **Review** · please, spell, number
- **Form and patterns** · Formulaic repair: Again, please. Slowly, please. Yes / No.
- **Guided practice** · Distinguish close-sounding codes, then use a cue card to repair a spoken mismatch.
- **L** · Correct one digit after a repeat requested by the learner.
- **S** · Request a slower repeat and confirm the corrected name or code.
- **R** · Identify which of two signup entries was corrected.
- **W** · Record the corrected entry on a short signup slip.

### `lq-home-plaza` · Find the bus and the bag (`home`, A1)

**Story** · Luz points out a clue. The player guides Sam and returns the lost bag.

**Transfer** · A neighborhood library entrance and a lost umbrella.

#### `lq-home-plaza.origins` · Introduce two people

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-start.name:form`, `lq-home-start.repair:s`
- **New** · he, she, friend, from, student
- **Review** · I, you, hello
- **Form and patterns** · am/are/is with I/you/he/she; affirmative, negative and origin question in successive practice.
- **Guided practice** · Pair fictional profiles with voices, swap subject cards and introduce the new profile.
- **L** · Match two people to their places of origin.
- **S** · Give your origin and introduce a friend using two subjects.
- **R** · Match a short profile to a name and origin.
- **W** · Write two profile facts with the correct verb for each subject.

#### `lq-home-plaza.people-things` · Describe people and things

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-plaza.origins:form`, `lq-home-start.spell:r`
- **New** · it, they, book, phone, small, happy, these, those
- **Review** · friend, student, one, two
- **Form and patterns** · it is / they are; this/that/these/those, regular plurals and basic adjective position.
- **Guided practice** · Sort caption pairs, then substitute pronouns or demonstratives as the picture changes.
- **L** · Match a singular and a plural description to two pictures.
- **S** · Identify one object and two people in a two-turn exchange.
- **R** · Distinguish singular and plural on a set of three labels.
- **W** · Write two captions that accurately distinguish singular and plural.

#### `lq-home-plaza.place` · Locate a place

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-plaza.origins:form`, `lq-home-start.spell:r`
- **New** · where, bus stop, park, next to, near, sign
- **Review** · please, number
- **Form and patterns** · Where is…? / It's next to…; teach inversion and it reference.
- **Guided practice** · Match point-of-reference phrases on a map and complete a two-person location gap.
- **L** · Locate a stop on a two-landmark map from audio.
- **S** · Ask where the stop is and give one usable location.
- **R** · Match the route number on a ticket to the right sign.
- **W** · Write a one-line location for a new visitor.

#### `lq-home-plaza.direction` · Give two directions

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-plaza.place:form`, `lq-home-plaza.place:r`
- **New** · left, right, straight, turn, entrance
- **Review** · park, bus stop, next to
- **Form and patterns** · Imperatives with Go/Turn and sequence with then.
- **Guided practice** · Trace modeled directions, order two commands and use a map with the destination hidden from a partner.
- **L** · Trace two instructions to the right endpoint.
- **S** · Guide Sam across two turns without pointing at the answer.
- **R** · Read two arrows and one entrance label.
- **W** · Write two ordered steps for an alternate starting point.

#### `lq-home-plaza.objects-map` · Place everyday objects

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-plaza.place:form`, `lq-home-plaza.people-things:form`
- **New** · under, between, in front of, shelf, bench
- **Review** · book, sign, these
- **Form and patterns** · in/on/under with concrete reference; between and in front of with one-step map practice.
- **Guided practice** · Move icons after a short instruction, then write clues for a second arrangement.
- **L** · Choose where two objects belong from short directions.
- **S** · Ask where two objects are and locate them verbally.
- **R** · Match three object notes to a simple layout.
- **W** · Write two location clues for a partner.

#### `lq-home-plaza.bag` · Identify who owns an object

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-plaza.objects-map:form`, `lq-home-plaza.people-things:form`
- **New** · bag, blue, red, mine, yours, lost
- **Review** · my name is, friend
- **Form and patterns** · a/an by vowel sound; adjective before noun; my/your vs mine/yours; What's this?
- **Guided practice** · Compare color and owner cards, rebuild noun phrases and ask a partner which claim matches.
- **L** · Distinguish two bag descriptions by owner and color.
- **S** · Ask about ownership and identify Sam's bag with two details.
- **R** · Match a lost-property note to the correct object.
- **W** · Write a short handover note with the object and owner.

### `lq-mexico-routines` · Routines and schedules (`mexico`, A1)

**Story** · A host helps the player choose a market visit around family schedules.

**Transfer** · Planning study or library time with a classmate.

#### `lq-mexico-routines.routine` · Describe your day

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-plaza.origins:form`
- **New** · work, study, live, get up, every day, breakfast
- **Review** · I, from, friend
- **Form and patterns** · Present simple with I/you/we/they; subject required; basic object position.
- **Guided practice** · Listen and order routine cards, then exchange a guided daily schedule.
- **L** · Put three weekday activities in heard order.
- **S** · Tell a host three things you do on a normal day.
- **R** · Find the work and study activities in a planner.
- **W** · Write three routine statements about a real or invented day.

#### `lq-mexico-routines.others` · Describe another person's routine

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-mexico-routines.routine:form`, `lq-home-plaza.origins:form`
- **New** · sister, brother, cook, read, usually, never
- **Review** · work, study, every day
- **Form and patterns** · Third-person -s/-es and adverb position; contrast /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/.
- **Guided practice** · Hear third-person endings, correct a schedule note and report a partner's two habits.
- **L** · Match two people to their routines and one frequency.
- **S** · Report one habit for each of two household members.
- **R** · Identify which activity never happens in two profiles.
- **W** · Write two contrasting third-person statements.

#### `lq-mexico-routines.time` · Read a timetable

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-home-start.spell:r`, `lq-mexico-routines.routine:form`
- **New** · o’clock, half past, noon, morning, afternoon, on Saturdays
- **Review** · breakfast, number, bus stop
- **Form and patterns** · Time numbers up to 59 by pattern; at + time, on + day, in + part of day.
- **Guided practice** · Discriminate spoken time contrasts, match clock faces and fill a weekly planner gap.
- **L** · Distinguish two teen/tens contrasts and recover day and hour.
- **S** · State two possible meeting times with their days.
- **R** · Find one open market slot in a weekly schedule.
- **W** · Write the day and time of the selected appointment.

#### `lq-mexico-routines.routine-ask` · Ask and agree on a time

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-mexico-routines.others:form`, `lq-mexico-routines.time:s`
- **New** · when, open, close, available, meet
- **Review** · on Saturdays, afternoon, read
- **Form and patterns** · Do/does in routine questions; don't/doesn't + base verb in replies.
- **Guided practice** · Rebuild do/does questions and exchange partly blank schedules to agree on a slot.
- **L** · Find the one available slot in a two-person exchange.
- **S** · Ask two questions including one about a third person and settle a time.
- **R** · Rule out a closed slot from two posted schedules.
- **W** · Confirm an agreed time and explain one unavailable slot.

### `lq-cartagena-food` · Food, help and alternatives (`colombia`, A1)

**Story** · At a language exchange, the player orders a meal and replaces an unavailable item.

**Transfer** · Selecting supplies for a neighborhood event.

#### `lq-cartagena-food.likes` · Ask about preferences

- **S** · Ask a partner about two foods and share one contrast.

#### `lq-cartagena-food.quantity` · Read quantities on a menu

- **Guided practice** · Match spoken orders to receipt lines, then complete a quantity and availability gap.
- **L** · Distinguish two orders by count and drink.
- **S** · Ask whether two items are available and state a quantity.
- **R** · Tell how many units there are from menu entries and a receipt.
- **W** · Write two order lines, one countable and one uncountable.

#### `lq-cartagena-food.prices` · Ask how much something costs

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-cartagena-food.quantity:form`, `lq-mexico-routines.time:l`
- **New** · price, dollar, cent, total, cost, how much
- **Review** · bread, water, some, number
- **Form and patterns** · How much is/are…?; demonstrative reference; hundreds and decimal prices in short chunks.
- **Guided practice** · Distinguish similar prices in audio, then buy from a priced list under a simple budget.
- **L** · Distinguish two prices with different tens and cents.
- **S** · Ask prices for two items and verify the total.
- **R** · Match three prices to items and identify the one within budget.
- **W** · Record two quantities, prices and the total for a purchase.

#### `lq-cartagena-food.order` · Order and substitute

- **Prerequisites** · `lq-cartagena-food.likes:form`, `lq-cartagena-food.quantity:s`, `lq-cartagena-food.prices:s`, `lq-home-start.repair:s`

- **S** · Order two items, accept or decline a substitute, then confirm.
