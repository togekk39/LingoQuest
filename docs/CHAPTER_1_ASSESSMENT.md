# Chapter 1 learner assessment

[Español (Latinoamérica)](CHAPTER_1_ASSESSMENT.es-419.md)

Use these facilitator-led forms to study learning in **La primera postal**. The six-question **Tu punto de partida** flow recommends a starting mission. Use the forms below at all three visits when measuring learning. Record outcomes outside the app as evidence for the chapter's specific objectives. The rubric has no CEFR threshold.

## Study procedure

1. Recruit learners who use Latin American Spanish and have little or no English. Include learners with different levels of device familiarity. Note prior English exposure and familiarity with the app before testing.
2. Give the first form before any Chapter 1 content, placement prompts, or reference materials. Then ask the learner to complete all three missions in story order on a fresh profile, starting at **Empezar mi viaje**. Let the learner use the app's own supports. Observe interruptions, recovery after leaving, and points where the learner requests help. If you intervene, record what you said or did.
3. Give the second form immediately after the chapter, before any extra practice led by the facilitator. Give the third seven days later (target window five to nine days). Record the actual interval plus any app review, other English practice, or repeated testing between visits. For an exploratory pilot, learners can keep using the app naturally. Report interim practice alongside delayed results.
4. Cycle participants evenly through form orders A–B–C, B–C–A, and C–A–B. Use that participant's assigned form once at each visit. Keep the same task order, Spanish directions, chosen accommodations, and audio delivery across visits. Pilot the three forms for difficulty and ambiguity before using them in a comparison study.

Allow about ten minutes per visit, with breaks and no time limit. Introduce the activities in Spanish. “Vas a usar un poco de inglés en situaciones cortas. Intenta responder con lo que ya sabes. Puedes pedir que repita las instrucciones en español, tomarte un descanso o pasar a la siguiente actividad. Si no sabes qué decir, está bien.” Explain the controls with an unrelated, unscored example using symbols. During scored items, repeat directions verbatim as needed and withhold translations, answer models, hints, and correctness feedback. If language help is given, keep the independent response and record the assisted attempt separately. Share item feedback after the delayed visit.

## Forms and prompts

Present tasks in row order. Prepare one card per task for the assigned form, with the Spanish directions and any English text for reading. Keep answer keys and audio scripts on the facilitator's copy. Text labeled _audio only_ is for the facilitator or a locally stored American English recording. Record or read each prompt at a consistent, comfortable pace. Allow up to one identical replay per listening item on request, and log replay use. Say the Spanish scenario without saying any English answer words. Use invented names throughout.

For task 3, the facilitator is the listener. Do not show a model or ask the learner to read English aloud for this item. An optional activity **after** all seven tasks can sample imitation. Play “It's red” or “It's blue,” then say “Ahora repite la frase.” Record whether a listener understood the color on first hearing, after a repeat, or could not determine it, with any audio-quality issue. Preserve this as a separate human observation. A transcript, a recording, and a listener's judgment each provide different information about the response. Accent strength carries no penalty.

## Scoring

Score first independent responses by item. Record what the learner meant to communicate and the target form separately. Ignore capitalization, punctuation, curly versus straight apostrophes, and accent when these do not change meaning or the target. Use the sample responses as anchors and accept other natural equivalents that meet the criteria.

| Task                    | Criteria and anchors                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Written introduction | Two separate marks, one for a greeting (`Hi`, `Hello`, `Hey`, etc.) and one for the assigned name with a subject and correct form of `be` (`I'm Mei`, `I am Mei`, `My name is Mei`). `Hi, Mei here` earns the greeting mark and communicates a name in the note. Record its natural alternative form separately.                                                                                                                                   |
| 2. Written direction    | One mark for the intended adjacent location. `next to`, `beside`, and `right by` can express the intended relationship. `near` alone leaves adjacency unspecified. A second mark requires the location message with an explicit subject and correctly formed `be`, as in `It is next to the bus stop` or `The backpack's beside the park` for the matching form. `Next to the park` communicates the location and earns one mark where applicable. |
| 3. Spoken description   | One mark for the correct color understood by the listener. A second mark requires that color in the intended statement with a clear subject and correctly formed `be`, such as `It's red` or `The bag is red` in A or C. Record partial responses such as “red” separately. If the evaluator could not observe the response, record unavailable. No automated speech or pronunciation grade enters this result.                                    |
| 4. Vocabulary recall    | One mark per correct English word in its requested position. Record a recognizable misspelling separately from an unrelated or missing word.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 5. Reading              | One mark for correct identity and one for correct color. Accept `blue`/`azul` and `red`/`rojo`/`roja` as applicable. An oral answer in Spanish is allowed and counts as reading comprehension of the supplied note.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 6. Listening            | One mark for the correct place named or described in English or Spanish. Accept regional equivalents such as `parada del autobús` and `paradero`. Record transcript-assisted performance separately as text-supported comprehension.                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 7. Dictation            | One content mark for hearing and representing both the color and `bag`. Note a recognizable spelling approximation. A second, sentence-form mark requires the complete dictated line with words in order and the correct article and verb. Accept `It's` and `It is` interchangeably. Report these marks under dictation.                                                                                                                          |

Classify responses as `independent`, `assisted`, `skip`, or `unavailable`. Score the independent response first and record any later correction separately. If a learner receives a valid presentation and then skips or answers only with language help, record zero independent marks and retain the status. An item prevented by equipment, an access barrier, or a missed visit remains unavailable and is excluded from the observed denominator with its reason reported. Note patterns like subject omission, missing or incorrect `be`, color confusion, article omission, and word order. Mark an error only when the response supplies evidence for that interpretation. For example, `Is red` omits the subject. A blank or isolated wrong noun gives too little evidence to assign a grammar error.

Offer zoom, adjustable type, keyboard access, extra time, and a screen reader for directions. Provide the spatial relationship in Spanish text even when a picture is used. For reading assessment, record screen-reader rendering of the English passage as an accommodation and report that result separately from silent reading. For a listener who needs the transcript, record the transcript path as text-supported. Score a live spoken response without a recording when possible. If a spoken response is declined or inaccessible, offer a written version and mark spoken evidence unavailable.

## Data and interpretation

Use a local coded record. Suggested fields are participant code, form/order, visit, date and lag, task ID, response or observer note, independent/assisted/skip/unavailable, individual marks, replays, accommodations, error type, and interim practice. Keep participant names out of the worksheet. Obtain agreement before observing the session or storing recordings, and follow appropriate consent procedures for minors. Keeping only the rubric and anonymized observations is sufficient for a first pilot.

Report participant counts and item denominators for each visit. Compare paired pre/post changes for independent writing and independent oral content, then compare the delayed form to both earlier visits. Report transfer items 2–3, vocabulary, reading, listening, and dictation separately. Describe missing visits, unavailable modalities, and assisted attempts. Examine whether form order or an individual prompt dominates the differences. A second teacher can score a sample without seeing visit order and resolve rubric ambiguities before using the results for decisions.

For usability, report how many first-time learners can find the chapter, interpret a task, complete it using only app supports, and resume after leaving. Review where they stall and whether the explanation helps them attempt the next task. For learning, the primary signal is increased independent production on unseen forms and evidence that it persists after the delay. An exploratory before-and-after pilot can identify design problems and estimate changes. A causal claim would need a comparison group and an appropriate study design. Automated tests verify app behavior. Learning outcomes require data from learners who complete the assessments.
